I. The Dynamic Immersion Principle

Translating into English in your head creates a cognitive bottleneck. When you learn that a word in another language "equals" an English word, your brain is forced to pass through English every time you hear or read it. True fluency requires direct sensory mapping: associating the sounds and script of the target language directly with situational reality.

"Acquisition occurs when the learner understands messages in the target language through meaningful situational contrasts, holding non-target variables invariant."

II. Controlled Minimal Contrasts

Standard language courses confound multiple variables at once: changing the noun, the verb, and the adjective in the same sentence. When that happens, the learner cannot tell which part of the sentence caused the ending to change. In KaizenLinguistics, every exercise isolates a single delta. The lexical roots remain strictly identical across all panels so your brain instinctively recognizes the inflectional morpheme.

III. TrueAccent Epistemic Guarantee

Modern AI apps frequently flatter learners by giving 95% scores on unintelligible speech. We reject false confidence. The TrueAccent acoustic pipeline enforces a 5-stage epistemic verification gate. If background noise, clipping, or mumbling makes it impossible to verify pronunciation with high confidence, the system returns Unable to Assess. Your card stability is protected, and you are prompted to try again in a quiet environment.

IV. Observation-Driven FSRS-6 Memory Scheduling

We use the 21-parameter Free Spaced Repetition Scheduler (FSRS-6) algorithm implemented in native Rust. Unlike legacy systems, we maintain four independent skill histories for every concept: Listening, Reading, Speaking, and Writing. Passing a listening card only proves you recognized the sound; your speaking score advances only when you speak aloud and pass acoustic verification.
